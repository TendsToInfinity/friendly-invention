# Acids, Bases and the pH Scale

Acids and bases are two important classes of chemical compounds. The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a solution is.

## The pH scale

pH runs from 0 to 14:

- pH 0 to 6: acidic (for example, lemon juice is about pH 2, and gastric acid is about pH 1 to 2).
- pH 7: neutral (pure water at 25 degrees Celsius).
- pH 8 to 14: basic or alkaline (for example, soap is about pH 9 to 10).

Each whole step on the pH scale represents a tenfold change in acidity. A solution of pH 3 is ten times more acidic than one of pH 4.

## Properties of acids

- Taste sour (never taste chemicals in the lab).
- Turn blue litmus paper red.
- React with metals to release hydrogen gas.
- React with bases in a neutralisation reaction.

## Properties of bases

- Taste bitter and feel soapy.
- Turn red litmus paper blue.
- Neutralise acids to form salt and water.

## Neutralisation

When an acid reacts with a base, they cancel each other out to form a salt and water:

acid + base --> salt + water

For example: HCl + NaOH --> NaCl + H2O

## Indicators

Indicators are substances that change colour in acids and bases. Litmus, methyl orange, and phenolphthalein are common indicators. Universal indicator gives a range of colours matching the whole pH scale.
