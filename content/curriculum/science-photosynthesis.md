# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria make their own food using light energy. It takes place mainly in the leaves, inside cell structures called chloroplasts that contain the green pigment chlorophyll.

## The balanced equation

Carbon dioxide and water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, produce glucose and oxygen:

6CO2 + 6H2O --light, chlorophyll--> C6H12O6 + 6O2

## Raw materials

- Carbon dioxide: enters the leaf through tiny pores called stomata.
- Water: absorbed by the roots and carried to the leaves through the xylem.
- Sunlight: captured by chlorophyll.
- Chlorophyll: the green pigment that absorbs light energy.

## The two stages

1. Light reactions (light-dependent): occur in the thylakoid membranes. Light energy splits water into hydrogen and oxygen. Oxygen is released as a by-product.
2. Calvin cycle (light-independent): occurs in the stroma. The hydrogen combines with carbon dioxide to form glucose.

## Why it matters

Photosynthesis is the source of almost all food and oxygen on Earth. It removes carbon dioxide from the air and releases the oxygen we breathe. Without it, food chains would collapse.

## Common exam points

- Chlorophyll is essential; leaves without it (variegated leaves) do not photosynthesise in the white regions.
- The starch test with iodine shows where photosynthesis has happened: a blue-black colour means starch is present.
- Factors affecting the rate: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature.
