# Verb Tenses

Tenses tell us when an action happens: in the past, the present, or the future. English has three main tenses, each with four aspects (simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous).

## The three simple tenses

- Past simple: "She went to school." Used for completed actions.
- Present simple: "She goes to school." Used for habits and general truths.
- Future simple: "She will go to school." Used for actions yet to happen.

## Third person singular rule

In the present simple, add -s or -es to the verb for he, she, or it: "He plays", "She goes", "It rains". A common error is writing "She go" instead of "She goes".

## Continuous (progressive) forms

Continuous forms describe an ongoing action, using a form of "to be" plus the -ing verb:

- Present continuous: "She is writing."
- Past continuous: "She was writing."

## Perfect forms

Perfect forms link two points in time, using "have" or "had" plus the past participle:

- Present perfect: "She has finished her homework."
- Past perfect: "She had finished before the bell rang."

## Tips for exams

- Keep tenses consistent within a paragraph unless the timeline genuinely changes.
- Watch subject-verb agreement, especially the third person singular -s.
- Practise by writing three sentences about your day in past, present, and future.
